While you type, the camera follows the letters. Cuttle records your screen, or Claude Code at work, and edits the take itself: it cuts the waiting, zooms on what matters, masks your secrets and frames it into a demo you can post. Everything runs on your computer.

## Download

- **Windows:** `Cuttle-0.1.12-win-x64.exe`. It installs for you alone, without admin rights, with Start menu and desktop shortcuts. It is not code-signed yet, so SmartScreen may not know it: choose *More info*, then *Run anyway*.
- **macOS (Apple silicon):** `Cuttle-0.1.12-mac-arm64.dmg`. Open it and drag Cuttle into Applications. The first recording asks for the screen, the microphone, input monitoring and accessibility.

Cuttle also needs ffmpeg: `winget install Gyan.FFmpeg` on Windows, `brew install ffmpeg` on a Mac. Its first start says what is missing.

Cuttle 0.1.1 to 0.1.11 update themselves to this version within a day, or at once with *Restart and update* (Settings, General).

## New in this version

- **The caret in Electron apps and browsers (Mac):**
  - A zoom on typing follows the caret, which macOS tells through its accessibility features. Electron apps (Claude, VS Code, Slack) and Chromium browsers (Chrome, Opera, Edge, Arc) show it only when they are asked, and Cuttle did not ask.
  - Now Cuttle asks each app as it comes to the front, and turns that back when the take ends.
  - Cuttle needs the Accessibility permission for this. If it lacks it, the recording picker says so, and macOS asks for it once.
- **Following the letters, not everything that changes:**
  - Where no caret tells, a zoom on typing follows what changes on the screen. It took everything that changed at once, so an answer streaming in elsewhere, or the blinking caret, pulled the camera back and forth.
  - Now it follows the place where the letters appear, starting at the field you clicked into.
- **A camera that glides along:**
  - While the text runs on, the view glides along with it and keeps it in view, instead of jumping every second. At a new line it goes back to the left.
  - While the text stays in view, the camera holds still.
