A new look: the video gets the room, and every setting is where you look for it. Cuttle records your screen, or Claude Code at work, and edits the take itself: it cuts the waiting, zooms on what matters, masks your secrets and frames it into a demo you can post. Everything runs on your computer.

## Download

- **Windows:** `Cuttle-0.1.13-win-x64.exe`. It installs for you alone, without admin rights, with Start menu and desktop shortcuts. It is not code-signed yet, so SmartScreen may not know it: choose *More info*, then *Run anyway*.
- **macOS (Apple silicon):** `Cuttle-0.1.13-mac-arm64.dmg`. Open it and drag Cuttle into Applications. The first recording asks for the screen, the microphone, input monitoring and accessibility.

Cuttle also needs ffmpeg: `winget install Gyan.FFmpeg` on Windows, `brew install ffmpeg` on a Mac. Its first start says what is missing.

Cuttle 0.1.1 to 0.1.12 update themselves to this version within a day, or at once with *Restart and update* (Settings, General).

## New in this version

- **The editor, redesigned:**
  - The panels are cards with room between them, and the video takes about 40 % of the window instead of 27 %.
  - The timeline shows only the rows that hold something, with taller lanes and calmer colours.
  - The tools float on the video's edge.
- **A layout of your own:**
  - Drag the inspector's edge to make it wider or narrower, and the timeline's top edge to make it taller or lower. A double click puts it back.
  - *Ctrl+I* (*⌘I* on a Mac), or the button in the title bar, hides the inspector, so the video gets even more room.
  - Cuttle keeps your layout for the next project.
- **The inspector:**
  - A selection's settings come in sections (Speed, Timing, Area, Movement…), and the units sit inside the fields.
  - Seconds into the take and pixels of the screen wait under *Advanced*.
  - In the Look tab, each part has its switch in its head and says what it is now. Sliders show their value, and colours take a hex code.
- **The recording picker:**
  - Its bar matches the bar you see while recording.
  - A permission Cuttle lacks gets its own line with *Open System Settings*, and the picker says why *Record* waits.
- **Settings:**
  - Each page has a title. On a Mac, a Permissions card shows what Cuttle may do; a Hotkeys card lists every recording hotkey.
  - On the first start, *Start recording* appears once everything is ready.
- **Smaller things:**
  - The launcher shows the Mac's keys (⌃⌥⇧R) on a Mac.
  - The command menu (*Ctrl+K*) has every action, the preview's formats too.
  - On a narrow window the timeline's bar folds to coloured dots; a block too small for its name shows its first letter.
