Claude Code's videos stop looking alike: looks of their own, motion blocks and Lottie animations, footage and sounds, shaders, and a review before the render. Cuttle records your screen, or Claude Code at work, and edits the take itself: it cuts the waiting, zooms on what matters, masks your secrets and frames it into a demo you can post. Everything runs on your computer.

## Download

- **Windows:** `Cuttle-0.3.5-win-x64.exe`. It installs for you alone, without admin rights, with Start menu and desktop shortcuts. It is not code-signed yet, so SmartScreen may not know it: choose *More info*, then *Run anyway*.
- **macOS (Apple silicon):** `Cuttle-0.3.5-mac-arm64.dmg`. Open it and drag Cuttle into Applications. The first recording asks for the screen, the microphone, input monitoring and accessibility.

Cuttle also needs ffmpeg: `winget install Gyan.FFmpeg` on Windows, `brew install ffmpeg` on a Mac. Its first start says what is missing.

Cuttle 0.1.1 to 0.3.4 update themselves to this version within a day, or at once with *Restart and update* (Settings, General).

## New in this version

- **The Claude skill stays current:** after every update Cuttle installs its skill for Claude Code again, without a click, so Claude has the new guides at once. A `cuttle` skill folder that Cuttle did not write is left alone.

## For Claude Code

- **A review before the render:** `cuttle review` measures what makes a video dull or hard to follow and answers with notes weighted P0 to P2, plus a sheet of the scenes. It looks for an empty first frame, scenes that all build up from nothing or look alike, a picture that stands still too long, text too small or too short-lived to read, a silent video, and one that looks like the last. MCP: `review`.
- **Looks of a video's own:** a look sets a medium's rules once for every page: its ground, its inks, its type, and how things come in and leave. Eight examples show how (`swiss`, `poster`, `editorial`, `data-desk`, `paper-cut`, `risograph`, `blueprint`, `zine`). They are not a menu: Claude finds each video's look in its subject, and `cuttle look new` writes the frame for it. `cuttle look list`, `show` and `apply` do the rest (MCP: `look_list`, `look_show`, `look_apply`, `look_new`).
- **Motion blocks, Lottie animations and icons:** `cuttle blocks search`, `show` and `add` find a block in HyperFrames' registry (about 200), a Lottie animation or one of Iconify's icons. Each can be looked at before it is used, then comes into the project, renders offline and the same every time, and has its licence noted in `CREDITS.md`.
- **Footage, photographs and sounds:** `cuttle media search`, `show` and `add` bring in footage and photographs from Pexels and Pixabay and sound effects from Freesound, each with its author and licence. You need your own free key (`PEXELS_API_KEY`, `PIXABAY_API_KEY` or `FREESOUND_API_KEY`), and Cuttle never writes it into a file. Footage and photographs can become a scene of their own.
- **A bigger motion kit:**
  - Timelines from anime.js, GSAP and Web Animations land on the video's frame.
  - `cuttle/shader` runs a fragment shader over the page, with a GLSL library of noise, grain, halftone, dithering and grading.
  - Ten transitions are drawn with it: wipe, zoom, whip, push, iris, glitch, pixels, liquid, flood and burn.
  - Lucide's icons (`cuttle motion icons`) and ten more fonts.

## Fixed in this version

- **The screenshot editor on Windows** could lose a change. When another program had the shot's file open for a moment (a virus scanner, OneDrive), the change was not written and nothing said so. It now waits for the file, and says so if it still cannot write.
- **+ Motion with a transition** in the editor put it on the clip as an overlay. It now goes in as the way into the scene under the playhead.
